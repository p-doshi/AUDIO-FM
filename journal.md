## 2026-08-09 — paradigm correction: music2vec is data2vec-family, not JEPA

- **Decision reversed.** music2vec has been labeled "JEPA-family" since the very first CLAUDE.md draft and carried that framing through every turn of Phase 1 analysis so far, including treating its cross-model RSA isolation as informative about H1 ("JEPA-family models agree with each other more than with others"). User caught this and walked through the actual architectural distinction: data2vec-style models (music2vec) have a student encoder that operates directly on masked input and predicts the EMA teacher's averaged top-K layer representations — no separate predictor network. JEPA as specified (and as A-JEPA/Audio-JEPA's own methods sections describe it) has three components: context encoder, EMA-updated target encoder, and a *separate predictor network* conditioned on the context representation. Both lineages descend from BYOL and are self-distillation-with-EMA-teacher, but the decoupled predictor is the real dividing line, and music2vec doesn't have one.
- **Practical consequence**: as of this correction there are zero working JEPA-family models in the active comparison. `audio_jepa` (the `ltuncay/Audio-JEPA` substitute, still deferred pending its Hydra-config loader work) will be the *first* JEPA-family data point once wired up, not a second one to compare against music2vec — H1 as originally phrased (mutual agreement *among* JEPA-family models) needs two working checkpoints and isn't testable yet.
- **Corrected everywhere the mislabel appeared**: `CLAUDE.md`'s candidate-model table and H1 statement (both annotated in place with correction notes, not silently rewritten), `audio_comp/models/music2vec.py`'s `ModelInfo.paradigm` field and docstring, `README.md`'s model table. Did NOT rewrite earlier journal entries above that say "JEPA-family" for music2vec — journal is append-only per CLAUDE.md's own convention; this entry is the correction of record.
- **New confound documented for future work**: `ltuncay/Audio-JEPA` is trained on meaningfully less compute than the other active teachers (100k steps, ~14h on 4 V100s, 5,338h AudioSet vs. wav2vec2/data2vec's 400k steps on larger batches), and its own paper reports it substantially underperforming both baselines on several linear-probe tasks (e.g. Speech Commands V1: 0.152 vs. data2vec's 0.927). If it comes back RSA-isolated once wired up, that's confounded between "JEPA-paradigm geometry is genuinely different" and "this checkpoint is comparatively undertrained" — RSA alone can't tell them apart. Documented directly in `audio_comp/models/audio_jepa.py`'s docstring so it's visible right where the loader gets implemented.
- **A specific, testable prediction for later**: the Audio-JEPA paper states its objective favors embedding cohesion over linear separability (strong kNN, weak linear probe on the same tasks) — predicting a *low* TwoNN intrinsic dimension and/or unusually tight within-category clustering once run through `inspect_geometry.py`. This is the opposite direction from music2vec, which had the *highest* ID of the six active models. A low-ID/high-cohesion result for `audio_jepa` would be real, paper-grounded evidence for paradigm-driven geometry differences — meaningfully stronger than an unexplained isolated RSA number. Not run yet — `audio_jepa` still needs its loader wired up first.

## 2026-08-10 (cont'd) — refining the null result before treating it as final

- User pushed back on the "geometry doesn't predict function" framing before letting it stand: (1) check whether X-ARES's kNN read (not just MLP) tells a different story for audio_jepa, since the source paper's own claim is specifically a kNN-vs-linear-probe split, not a flat "worse at everything"; (2) CLAP winning deserves a real hypothesis (discriminative/contrastive pretraining pressure, à la Wang & Isola alignment/uniformity), not just a note, and a silhouette-score proxy on the RDMs already computed would test it cheaply; (3) wav2vec2's LibriSpeech-ASR zero, given how architecturally close it is to hubert (their tightest RSA pair, 0.80), smells more like a wiring bug than a real result, worth an actual diff before shelving. Right call on all three — the flat null was underspecified.
- **(1) kNN vs MLP rank check**: audio_jepa's *relative rank* improves under kNN vs. MLP on both X-ARES tasks — FMA 4th→3rd (passes mert), UrbanSound8K 6th→5th (passes hubert). Real and direction-consistent with the paper's cohesion/separability split, though modest — not a reversal, audio_jepa doesn't become a top performer under kNN either. The kNN/MLP *ratio* tells a more complicated story: CLAP has the highest ratio of all 7 (0.94 avg — its kNN score is almost as good as its trained MLP score), not audio_jepa (0.69, 3rd). A high ratio means kNN captures most of what training can achieve — CLAP isn't trading cohesion for separability, it's winning at both simultaneously, which argues against reading the ratio itself as a clean cohesion indicator.
- **(2) Silhouette score on our own probe set** (not X-ARES's — the 5-category, 10,000-clip RDM data from Stage 0): **audio_jepa has by far the highest silhouette of all 7 models (0.602 — nearly double CLAP's 0.313, which comes 2nd)**. This is a much sharper version of the 2026-08-09 `inspect_geometry.py` finding (separation index 1.36, highest of the three checked then) now confirmed against all 7 models with a standard metric. So on *coarse* 5-domain discrimination (music/speech/bird/ship/city), audio_jepa's cohesion advantage is real and dominant, not marginal.
  - Correlated this silhouette ranking against X-ARES's average MLP score (FMA+UrbanSound8K) across all 7 models: **Spearman rho = 0.357, p = 0.432 — weak and not statistically significant** (n=7, so likely underpowered, but the direction and magnitude are informative regardless). audio_jepa is the clearest violator: #1 on coarse silhouette, only 6th of 7 on average X-ARES MLP.
  - **Refined finding, replacing the flat null from the earlier entry**: audio_jepa's cohesion advantage is real, large, and specific to *coarse* domain-level discrimination — it does not transfer to the *fine-grained*, within-domain classification X-ARES actually tests (8 FMA genres, 10 UrbanSound8K event types). "Geometry doesn't predict function" was imprecise; the more accurate statement is "coarse-grained cluster cohesion doesn't predict fine-grained linear classification within a domain, and these are measuring different things."
  - CLAP's fine-grained dominance (best MLP on both X-ARES tasks) despite only 2nd-place *coarse* silhouette supports the user's alignment/uniformity hypothesis better than a raw ID/cohesion story does: contrastive training seems to produce representations specifically suited to fine-grained discriminability in a way TwoNN dimension and coarse silhouette don't capture. Not confirmed with a real alignment/uniformity computation yet (Wang & Isola metrics, or a per-class silhouette breakdown) — flagged as the actual next step if this becomes a formal Stage 6 diagnostic candidate, more promising than raw ID alone.
- **(3) wav2vec2 wiring diff**: instantiated both `HubertXaresEncoder` and `Wav2Vec2XaresEncoder` and compared their *measured* (not assumed) properties directly — **identical**: `sampling_rate=16000, output_dim=1024, hop_size_in_ms=20.101` for both. Wrapper code was already confirmed byte-identical apart from the model name (2026-08-10 earlier entry). This rules out a dimension/frame-rate wiring bug definitively — the two encoders feed the Qwen2 ASR decoder structurally identical input. wav2vec2's `Wav2Vec2Model` load report shows several `UNEXPECTED` keys (`quantizer.*`, `project_q.*`, `project_hid.*`) — normal and expected, those are the contrastive-pretraining head's parameters, correctly dropped when loading the plain encoder-only class, not a sign of a bad checkpoint load. **Downgraded from "possible bug, unresolved" to "confirmed not a wiring/config bug, cause still unknown"** — most likely a genuine property of wav2vec2-large-lv60's specific embedding content interacting badly with X-ARES's ASR training for this exact checkpoint, not something in our integration. Not chased further; would need to inspect actual Qwen2 decoder outputs/attention during training to go deeper, which is a bigger undertaking than this check.
  - **Follow-up check, same session**: user specifically flagged `do_normalize`/checkpoint-variant matching (a known HF wav2vec2 gotcha — layer-norm "large/robust" checkpoints need `attention_mask` passed for padded batches, or CTC-style decoding degrades badly while pooled classification barely notices) as worth checking harder. `do_normalize=True` + `feat_extract_norm=layer` was already confirmed correctly paired for wav2vec2-large-lv60 (2026-08-09). Checked the more specific angle: `do_stable_layer_norm` — **identical between hubert (`True`) and wav2vec2 (`True`)**, so it doesn't differentiate them, but checking it surfaced something more concrete: read X-ARES's own `audiowebdataset.py`/`task.py` source directly — its collate function *does* zero-pad variable-length clips into batches, then the encoding loop unpacks the result as `(audio, _)`, **explicitly discarding the lengths tensor** before ever calling any encoder. No model — ours or any other X-ARES-evaluated encoder — ever receives an attention mask from this framework. Real, confirmed, framework-level mechanism, architecturally exactly the risk the user described — but since hubert shares the identical `do_stable_layer_norm=True` flag and scored 0.855 anyway, it doesn't cleanly explain a hubert-vs-wav2vec2 *difference* on its own. Every configuration axis now checked (wrapper code, measured output_dim/hop_size, do_normalize, feat_extract_norm, do_stable_layer_norm) comes back identical between the two models. Genuinely closing this out at "confirmed not a config/wiring mismatch, real but non-differentiating framework-level padding risk identified" — resolving further would need actually implementing attention-mask support (X-ARES doesn't give us the lengths to do this properly without a heuristic) and re-running both models to compare, which is a bounded but separate piece of work, not pursued now.
